# AI tells in English: CV, cover letter and interview guide

Trimmed and adapted from the original English checklist. Last review: 2026-10. These lists age fast; review them about every 6 months.

**Important:** every replacement must be at or below the language level confirmed in the CV. At B1: short sentences, common verbs, no idioms. The "plain" column below is written for that level.

## 1. Always rewrite
**From the project spec:**
- passionate, passionate about
- leverage, leveraged
- spearheaded
- proven track record
- dynamic
- thrilled, excited to apply, excited about the opportunity

**More CV and letter tells:**
- results-driven, result-oriented, team player, fast learner, self-starter, detail-oriented (without a fact next to it)
- hit the ground running, go-getter, think outside the box
- "I am writing to express my interest in…", "I believe I would be a great fit"
- "I look forward to hearing from you", "thank you for your time and consideration", "I would welcome the opportunity"
- seamless, robust, comprehensive, innovative, cutting-edge, holistic, synergy
- demonstrated/proven ability to…

## 2. Plain replacements (B1-friendly)
| AI word | Plain choice |
|---|---|
| delve into | look at |
| leverage, utilize | use |
| foster | build |
| facilitate | help |
| navigate (a problem) | deal with |
| ensure | make sure |
| spearheaded, orchestrated | built, made, worked on (the verb must match what the CV says) |
| streamline | make faster, make simpler (only if the CV says so) |
| crucial, vital, pivotal | delete, or say why it matters |
| robust, comprehensive | delete, or say what it covers |

If no plain replacement fits the CV facts, delete the phrase.

## 3. Hedges and filler
- "It is important to note that…", "It is worth mentioning…", "Needless to say…"
- "In many cases", "Generally speaking"
- "Studies show…", "Many companies…", "Experts agree…"

Cut the hedge and state the fact. If the claim has no source in the CV, cut the whole sentence.

## 4. Structure and rhythm
- Lists of three in every paragraph ("fast, reliable and efficient").
- "Not only X but also Y".
- Slogan-like lines ("turning ideas into solutions").
- Every sentence the same length; every paragraph built the same way.
- A closing that repeats the opening.

## 5. Punctuation
- Em dash (—): none in the CV, one at most in the letter, none in the interview questions.
- Bold on many phrases, decorative emojis: none in the CV or letter.

## 6. Tone
- False warmth ("I hope this letter finds you well").
- Enthusiasm with no fact behind it.

## 7. CV-specific
- Bullets that start with "Responsible for…" or "Worked on…" and never say what was done.
- Adjectives about the person with no fact next to them.

Fix: a bullet starts with a past-tense verb that says what was done, with what, and what came out. If the CV has no result, do not invent one: keep the action.

## 8. How to decide
1. Is there a concrete fact in the CV or profile to put instead? → replace.
2. Is it a quality with no support? → cut.
3. Would fixing it need an invented fact? → cut and note it in `sin_arreglar`.
